Direct labels, round 2

Phase 1 technical spike, revised after your review of the spike findings.

What changed

  • Axis titles are semibold.
  • The plot box is 1.1 wide to 1 high in every graph.
  • Font sizes, line widths and marker sizes scale with the figure's width, so the narrow journal figure keeps the same proportions as the full-size one.
  • Series labels use a new method, described below, built to your rule: inside the graph, next to their own series, overlapping neither each other nor any series.

How labels are placed

  1. Inside the plot. Labels never sit in the margins.
  2. Next to their own series. A label's own series must be the one nearest to it, and close by. Each label is also drawn in its series' colour.
  3. Touching nothing. A label keeps a clear gap from every line and every marker, its own included, and from every other label.
  4. In open space. Among the positions that pass these rules, the method prefers ones close to the label's own series and far from the others, with a slight preference for the start or end of the line.
  5. Room past the line ends, if needed. When no position passes, the x-axis grows by at most 15% so that labels can sit just past the ends of their lines.
  6. Legend as a last resort. When series are so close together that no label can clearly belong to one of them, the graph uses a legend instead.

The method is deterministic: the same data always gives the same placement.

Your decisions

1. Is this placement good enough to be the default for xy? Comment on any graph where a label looks wrong.

2. Rule 5: is growing the x-axis by up to 15% acceptable before falling back to a legend? The alternative is to go straight to the legend.

3. Rule 6: is a legend the right fallback for series that can't be told apart?
