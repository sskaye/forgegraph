Spike findings

Phase 1 technical spike. The spike tested the riskiest technical assumptions before the format specifications commit to them. It is throwaway code on branch phase-01-spike and is never merged.

Your decision: direct-label style

Which direct-label style or styles should the xy chart type define? Comment on this page with your choice. The styles are compared under "Direct-label styles" below.

Recommendation: define both margin and end, with margin as the default. Margin is the robust choice. End gives the look of your examples; it gains avoidance of other lines, and falls back to margin, then to a legend, when its labels don't fit.

What the spike found

#FindingWhat the specifications will say
1Without pyplot, a figure has no drawing canvas, so text can't be measured.The engine attaches a canvas whenever it creates a figure.
2Setting only some maths font families leaves matplotlib searching system fonts for the rest, with a risk of mixed typefaces.The style specification sets every maths font family to the theme font.
3The same sentence appears in every catalog tab. Re-attaching by text alone put a comment on the wrong tab.The catalog build picks the occurrence whose surrounding text matches what Forgemark recorded with the comment. Tested: it lands on the right tab.
4More series than palette colours.The chart-type specification defines the rule: colours repeat and lint warns.
5Forgemark's command-line tool isn't published as a package: the npm name belongs to someone else, and Forgemark releases ship only the desktop app. The catalog build needs it in CI. Options: keep a pinned copy of the bundled tool in this repo, or publish the bundled tool with each Forgemark release and download it by version. The second is cleaner but is a change to Forgemark.
6The 8-series crowded case correctly fell back to a legend. No change needed.

Direct-label styles

Each style is shown on the same two datasets: four cells with a benchmark, and five converging runs.

End

Each label sits next to its line's last point, as in your example graphs. Labels are spread apart so they never overlap each other, and the plot falls back to a legend when they can't fit.

Verdict: Reads naturally. Two problems: labels avoid each other but not other lines (Cell A sits on the Benchmark line), and making room by widening the x-axis wastes space (the axis runs to 175).

Margin

Labels sit in the right margin, outside the plot, at the height where each line ends. They are spread apart the same way.

Verdict: Never covers data, doesn't widen the axes, and stacks cleanly when lines converge. Labels sit further from lines that end early (Cells A, B and C end at 100, the labels are past 140).

Library: matplotlib-label-lines

Labels are drawn along each line, in a white box.

Verdict: Labels cover the lines they name, and in the crowded case they cover each other's lines. Not recommended.

Library: adjustText

Labels start at each line's end and are pushed apart iteratively.

Verdict: Labels still overlap lines, and in the crowded case they pile up unreadably. Not recommended.
