Tick algorithm comparison

The catalog baseline with each published tick algorithm, at three plot widths (height auto). Each algorithm gets the same target number of labels, from the axis's length: about one per three tick-label heights along x and two along y. Log axes keep their own rule.

Extended Wilkinson

Talbot, Lin & Hanrahan, IEEE InfoVis 2010. Scores every candidate on simplicity, coverage of the data, closeness to the target number of labels, and legibility; ggplot2's default.

Wilkinson

Wilkinson, The Grammar of Graphics, 2005. Scores granularity, roundness and coverage over label counts from half to six times the target.

Heckbert

Heckbert, "Nice Numbers for Graph Labels", Graphics Gems, 1990. A nice range, then a nice step of 1, 2 or 5 times a power of ten.

Nelder (AS 96)

Nelder, "A Simple Algorithm for Scaling Graphs", Applied Statistics, 1976. Steps from 1, 1.2, 1.6, 2, 2.5, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, scale centred on the data.

R pretty()

Base R graphics' rule: 1, 2, 5 or 10 times a power of ten, favouring 1 and 10.

matplotlib

Matplotlib's default: as many ticks as fit, steps 1, 2, 2.5, 5 or 10 times a power of ten. What ForgeGraph used until now.
